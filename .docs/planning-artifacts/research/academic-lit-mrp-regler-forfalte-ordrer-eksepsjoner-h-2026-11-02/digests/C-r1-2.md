# Digest C-r1-2: ERP documentation on horizon vs cumulative lead time, low-level codes, and past-dated requirements

Researcher brief: C7 and C9, plus ASCM dictionary definitions. Accessed 2026-11-02. I spent the whole budget (15 tool calls). I fetched 6 distinct pages in full; one of them, the SAP low-level-code page, would not render. The other findings come from search-result excerpts and are marked as such.

## Findings

1. **D365 SCM: the coverage time fence *is* the planning horizon, and the docs say it must exceed total lead time.** "Planners can define the planning horizon (the coverage time fence in days)". "To help avoid delays, you must ensure that the coverage time fence is longer than the total lead time. The default system value is 100 days." The fence is counted in calendar days from the planning-run date. | Coverage time fences – Supply Chain Management | Microsoft Learn | ms.date 2026-07-27, updated 2026-10-09 | accessed 2026-11-02 | https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/master-planning/planning-optimization/coverage-time-fence | high | erp-behaviour | primary

2. **D365: supply and demand outside the fence are dropped, not flagged.** "The system doesn't generate requirement transactions for any supply and demand that falls outside the coverage time fence." Approved supply and demand outside the fence are not loaded, so "they don't trigger any replenishment, and the system doesn't calculate delays." Because the fence filters only input, "the resulting planned orders might have a date that is after today's date plus the coverage time fence." | same source as 1 | high | erp-behaviour | primary

3. **D365 Planning Optimization: lower-level (derived) requirements outside the fence depend on the version and a feature flag.** In 10.0.48 and earlier, "Planning Optimization doesn't filter derived requirements by coverage time fence." From 10.0.49 there is an optional feature, *Filter derived requirements by coverage time fence*. With it on, Planning Optimization "doesn't replenish derived requirements that fall outside the coverage time fence that applies to them". One example is "Planned BOM/formula component issue". The component's own fence applies, looked up in this order: item coverage, then coverage group, then the global setting. So by default, BOM-component demand from a parent inside the fence is still planned even if it lands outside the component's fence. | same source as 1 | high | erp-behaviour | primary

4. **D365: the docs give no guidance that the explosion fence should cover cumulative lead time.** The Master plans overview defines coverage, freeze, firming, forecast plan, capacity, action message, calculated delays and sequencing fences. The page I fetched has no section on an "explosion" fence. A search-result excerpt (not fetched in full) says that an explosion fence of 0 on a coverage group stops BOM explosion for that item, so it is treated as purchased ("Set up products that can be produced or procured", learn.microsoft.com). The capacity fence does relate to lead time: "If the requirement date for a planned production order is outside the capacity time fence, the lead time is determined by the delivery time of the item." | Master plans overview – Supply Chain Management | Microsoft Learn | ms.date 2026-03-23, updated 2026-09-01 | accessed 2026-11-02 | https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/master-planning/master-plans | high for capacity fence and for the absence of explosion guidance on this page; low for the explosion-fence-zero behaviour (excerpt only) | erp-behaviour | primary

5. **D365: past-dated requirements are always processed, and orders in the past can be blocked by a setting.** "The coverage time fence is calculated forward from the current date. Master planning always processes requirements that occur before the current date." There is an option "Ensure that the planned orders are not created prior to master planning run date – Set this option to Yes to help guarantee that orders can't be scheduled for dates in the past." On delays: "If a planned order can't be fulfilled for the requested date, the system plans the order for the earliest fulfillment date ... based on lead times, and material and capacity availability". Separate options let you "Add the calculated delay to the requirement date". The page does not say directly how a component's requirement date is set when the parent is pushed forward. My unverified belief is that it follows the parent's rescheduled start. | same source as 4 | high (settings); unverified (component dating) | erp-behaviour | primary

6. **D365: the "Positive days" guidance ties a setting to lead time and the horizon.** "As a rule, set the positive days to a number that's between the longest lead time of the items and the coverage time fence." This implies that horizon >= longest lead time. | same source as 4 | high | erp-behaviour | primary

7. **D365: the planning run is organised by BOM level, numbered top-down.** A search-result excerpt from "Monitor a master planning run" (not fetched in full) says: "Coverage planning – BOM levels are shown as expected. They are calculated from the top down." End item = 0, subcomponents = 1, 2. Delays show the level times -1, and auto-firming shows 999 minus the level. The same excerpts say delays propagate up the BOM, but "only the items directly above the item causing the delay are updated with a delay during the master planning run". Business Central uses "Planning Level", where 0 = finished item. | Monitor a master planning run / Delays | Microsoft Learn | undated in excerpt | accessed 2026-11-02 | https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/master-planning/tasks/monitor-master-planning-run ; https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/master-planning/delays | medium | erp-behaviour | primary (excerpt only)

8. **SAP ERP: the low-level code is the lowest usage level and sets the planning sequence.** Search-result excerpt; the page itself did not render (it is JS-only): "The low-level code represents the lowest level of usage of a material within all product structures. It determines the sequence in which the materials are planned. The system plans materials with the low-level code 0 first and then those with 1 and so on." A second SAP page, on MRP areas in S/4HANA, says planning "has to be completed for one low-level code before the system commences the planning of the next low-level code". Together these support "plan a common part once, after all its parents". I found no explicit SAP sentence saying the gross dependent requirements are combined; that is my inference from the sequencing rule. | Calculating Low-Level Codes (SAP ERP); Process flow for MRP with MRP Areas | SAP Help Portal | version not visible | accessed 2026-11-02 | https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_ERP/85d3fce10e264972a0155c8b46ecf93b/a797b6535fe6b74ce10000000a174cb4.html ; https://help.sap.com/docs/SUPPORT_CONTENT/mrp/3363506616.html | medium | erp-behaviour / definition | primary (excerpt only)

9. **SAP: backward scheduling that lands in the past switches to forward scheduling from today.** SCM/SNC 7.0 page: "If the backward scheduling leads to a date/time that lies in the past, the system conducts a forward scheduling. ... The scheduling starts on the current date/time (today)." Search-result excerpts from SAP ERP pages agree. "Backward Scheduling for In-House Production" says MRP switches to forward scheduling only when the start date is in the past. "Scheduling Collective Orders" says that when the start date is in the past the system switches to "Today" scheduling and logs a warning. One integration excerpt says the dependent requirement date for components falls on the order start date. So if the start date is moved to today, the child's requirement should land today, not in the past. That last step is my inference; I did not find it stated for classic MRP. | Scheduling of Planned Receipts | SAP Help (saphelp_snc70) | 7.0 | accessed 2026-11-02 | https://help.sap.com/doc/saphelp_snc70/7.0/en-US/b4/2b8453b794f763e10000000a4450e5/content.htm?no_cache=true ; https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_ERP/85d3fce10e264972a0155c8b46ecf93b/91abce5314894208e10000000a174cb4.html ; https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_ERP/bfece09273bd474d82fdd97bae070c25/cd04b753128eb44ce10000000a174cb4.html | high for the APO/SNC page; medium for the ERP excerpts | erp-behaviour | primary

10. **SAP: "planning horizon" is a net-change scope, not a demand cut-off.** Processing keys are NETCH (net change), NEUPL (regenerative) and NETPL ("net change planning in planning horizon"). The DRP doc says: "Only changes relevant to requirements planning that fall within the user-defined planning horizon are included in the planning run." It also recommends a regular total planning run. My unverified belief, not confirmed here, is that SAP sizes this horizon to cover at least the procurement and production lead times. | MRP Control Parameters Area; Running DRP | SAP Help Portal | S/4HANA on-premise (version not visible) | accessed 2026-11-02 | https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/7d9b296b529e4ef7baba080cf81124ec/4367b65334e6b54ce10000000a174cb4.html | medium (excerpt only) | erp-behaviour | primary

11. **Odoo 19: lead times chain backward from the deadline, and the "horizon" is a look-ahead for reordering rules, not an MRP horizon.** "Lead times are calculated in the order provided above, working backward from the delivery deadline". The order is customer lead time, sales security lead time, shipping policy, manufacturing lead time, days to prepare MO, vendor lead time, days to purchase. "Days to prepare manufacturing order" is "The number of days needed to replenish components, or manufacture sub-assemblies", and it can be calculated from the BoM components' lead times. From the search excerpt of the Replenishment report docs: "Forecasted date = Current date + Vendor Lead Time + Horizon Days". The visible text does not say what happens if the computed date is in the past; it only says "Odoo issues a warning message if the set delivery date is earlier than the expected date". | Lead times — Odoo 19.0 documentation; Replenishment report (18.0/19.0/20.0) | Odoo S.A. | 19.0 | accessed 2026-11-02 | https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/inventory/warehouses_storage/replenishment/lead_times.html | high (lead times); medium (horizon formula, excerpt only) | erp-behaviour | primary

12. **Cumulative lead time, as a vendor definition that is not ASCM's.** JD Edwards 9.2: "Cumulative lead time is the number of workdays that are required to acquire items and complete a product, from its lowest-level components to the final item." Also: "Cumulative lead time is the level lead time for a product plus the longest cumulative lead time of any of its components." And: "The cumulative lead time for a purchased item is its level lead time." In short it is the longest path, which is the critical-path idea. | Cumulative Lead Time | Oracle (JD Edwards EnterpriseOne 9.2) | 9.2 | accessed 2026-11-02 | https://docs.oracle.com/en/applications/jd-edwards/supply-chain-manufacturing/9.2/eoapd/cumulative-lead-time.html | high | definition | primary (vendor)

13. **Horizon >= cumulative lead time, from secondhand sources that echo APICS.** A Cargoz glossary says: "It should be long enough to accommodate the longest cumulative lead time for any item in the population". This closely matches my unverified recollection of the APICS planning-horizon entry, but it is not confirmed. A Chulalongkorn thesis says "planning horizon should be at least as long as the longest cumulative lead time for the item to be scheduled in MPS." In the search excerpts, Infor LN docs say the planning horizon must be >= the order horizon, which must cover the total cumulative order lead time. An Oliver Wight glossary defines cumulative lead time as "the longest length of time involved to accomplish the activity in question". | cargoz.com glossary; cuir.car.chula.ac.th thesis; docs.infor.com LN 10.x; oliverwight-americas.com | various | accessed 2026-11-02 | https://www.cargoz.com/glossary/planning-horizon-1506 ; https://docs.infor.com/ln/10.8/en-us/lnolh/cpordplanug/cpom000316.html ; https://www.oliverwight-americas.com/glossary-terms/cumulative-lead-time/ | low-medium | definition | secondhand (Infor = primary vendor, excerpt only)

## Disagreements between sources

- **What "planning horizon" means.** In D365 it is a hard demand and supply cut-off: the coverage fence. In SAP it is a net-change processing scope (NETPL); demand beyond it is not dropped. In Odoo, "horizon days" only extend the look-ahead for reordering rules. Do not treat the word as having one meaning across systems.
- **Lower-level requirements outside the horizon.** D365 by default (<=10.0.48, or with the feature off) still plans BOM-component demand derived from a parent inside the fence. With the 10.0.49+ feature on, D365 drops it when it falls outside the component's own fence. So even within one vendor the behaviour is configurable.
- **BOM level numbering.** D365 counts top-down (0 = end item) for coverage. Its delay and firming views use transformed numbers (-level, 999-level). SAP's low-level code is the *lowest* usage level, i.e. the maximum depth. These agree for a pure tree but differ in how a shared part is displayed.
- **Past dates.** SAP (SNC/APO, ERP "Today" scheduling) forward-schedules from today. D365 "always processes requirements that occur before the current date" and only blocks past-dated planned orders if a setting is on. So the default behaviour can differ.
