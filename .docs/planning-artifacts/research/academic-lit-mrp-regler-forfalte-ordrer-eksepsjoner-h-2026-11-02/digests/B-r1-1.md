2. **Message family in planner training: exception messages flag "rescheduling, expediting, delaying, or canceling orders". "'Reschedule-in' means accelerating an order; 'reschedule-out' means delaying it; and 'cancel' eliminates it altogether."** Common MRP outputs listed: "planned orders, pegging reports, exception messages, shortage lists, and rescheduling notices." | CPIM Top 20 Concepts – Using and Managing MRP | Fhyzics (CPIM training vendor) | n.d. | accessed 2026-11-02 | https://pages.fhyzics.net/hubfs/CPIM-Top-20-Concepts-Using%20and%20Managing%20MRP.pdf | medium-low: training vendor, no citations | definition | secondhand (summarises the ASCM CPIM body of knowledge)
3. **Firm planned orders "prevent automatic rescheduling that could cause nervousness"**. Ways to reduce nervousness are listed as "time fences, firm planned [orders]…". "Inside time fences, schedule changes are restricted to protect stability… beyond fences, MRP can reschedule more freely." 5. **Time fence = "Series of time intervals during which order changes are allowed or restricted; the nearest fence is most restrictive". 8. **Dampening of reschedule-out (ERP behaviour): no reschedule-out is generated if the order arrives early by less than "acceptable early days". **Synthesis against the hypothesis (inference, not evidence):**
- **B4.** Across every source, the recurring core is release (planned order / past due), reschedule in, reschedule out, and cancel. Decrease quantity and expedite also appear. No retrieved source names "lead time exceeds horizon" or a "safety stock violation" message. No retrieved source states a "minimum useful set"; that remains unanswered.
- **B5.** In Oracle and SAP (and implicitly Deltek), a late open order produces a reschedule-in message on the existing receipt, and the receipt still counts as covering the requirement. The SAP snippet says this explicitly. That matches the "move, don't duplicate" principle. An app that both creates a planned order and flags "receipt late" would therefore double-cover. This last step is my inference; I have not seen a source say it.
- **B6.** Noise control is evidenced only at the ERP and training level: day-tolerances/filters (Oracle, Deltek, SAP), firm planned orders, time fences, the action bucket, and pegging/BURP done by the planner.

## Disagreements between sources
- **Which side is damped.** Oracle damps only reschedule-out ("always generates a reschedule in"). Deltek's filter applies to all reschedule messages, and SAP uses a tolerance window for both directions (snippet). The tolerance can therefore be one-sided or two-sided.
- **Codes and granularity.** SAP separates firmed (10) from unfirmed (30) receipts. Deltek collapses in and out into a single "Reschedule Order" with signed change days and adds "Decrease Quantity". Oracle and Infor use in/out (move-in/move-out).
- **Release and expedite.** Oliver Wight lists "Release Order" as an action message. Deltek has none and uses "Planned Order" with an inside-lead-time flag. CPIM lists expediting separately from reschedule-in. Sage (snippet) treats expedite as severe lateness and reschedule-in as mild.
