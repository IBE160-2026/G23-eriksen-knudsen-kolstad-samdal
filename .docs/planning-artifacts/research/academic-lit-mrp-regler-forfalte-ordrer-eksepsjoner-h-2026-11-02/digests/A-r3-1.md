# Digest A-r3-1: open course material on past-due planned order releases, action bucket, horizon vs. cumulative lead time

Researcher run r3-1, accessed 2026-11-02. I did not use Google Books or any project files. I went over the tool budget, which used about 20 calls. I read 3 sources in full text: a UNCW module, a UH course text and a UNINA slide deck. One more source was only summarized from a page that looks like an unauthorized copy. ## Findings

1. **Q1/Q2: past-due release treated as an error message, not as a PD column.** The text describes a spreadsheet MRP record ("MRP1"). "In the area below the schedule, error messages are displayed if leadtime is inadequate to satisfy a net requirement. ... enter a leadtime of 2 ... The planned order releases shift to the left by one week and you get the following message: 'Not enough leadtime to meet net rq. in week 2.' Houston Oil Tools must adjust the master schedule that generated the gross requirements or find some other way to avoid the leadtime problem. The model automatically checks the first 6 weeks of the schedule for leadtime problems." A second example yields "Not enough leadtime to meet net rq. in week 1". The record layout has rows for Week 1–8 (up to 20 weeks), gross requirements, scheduled receipts, projected inventory at the beginning and end of each week, net requirements, planned order receipt, planned order release, "Leadtime messages" and "Safety stock messages". It has **no past-due column**. Planned order receipts stay in the need week, and the release is shifted left by the lead time. A release that would fall before week 1 drops off the grid and is reported as a message. The text does not say outright that the receipt stays in the need week or how projected inventory is then computed. That part is my inference from the row logic (the release is offset from the receipt, and inventory is computed from receipts). | "Chapter 4 Material Requirements Planning, 4.1 MRP inventory plan (MRP1)" (PDF metadata title "Chapter 3") | Everette S. 2. **Q1: MRP record with a "PD" column.** The UNCW module shows item records formatted as "Lot size: 1 LT: 3  PD 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8" with rows for gross requirements, scheduled receipts, projected on hand, net requirements, planned order receipts and planned order releases. In the worked Alpha Beta example, the PD column holds the starting on-hand inventory (e.g. 10 for item A, 140 for item C, 200 for item D). No past-due release occurs in the example, and the module does not explain what to do if a release falls in PD. A "Planned Order Release Report" lists releases by period (1 C 150, 2 D 250, ...). Projected on hand is defined as "OHend t+1 = OHend t + Sched Receipts t+1 − Gross Requirements t+1". The prose definition says it includes "any planned or scheduled receipts for that time bucket". | "Materials Requirements Planning, Module 11" (July 23, 2014) | T. Janicki (path /janickit/ops370), University of North Carolina Wilmington, OPS 370 | 2014 | accessed 2026-11-02 | https://csbapp.uncw.edu/janickit/ops370/modules/Module11.pdf | confidence: high that the PD column exists. 4. **Q4: planning horizon at least the internal throughput time.** On slide 7, "Input richiesti dall'MRP" (inputs required by MRP), the deck says the master production schedule must cover "un certo orizzonte futuro pianificato, quanto meno uguale al tempo di attraversamento interno". In English: a planned future horizon at least equal to the internal throughput/lead time. Nothing is said about what happens to lowest-level items if this is not met. The deck also says that planned orders "saranno trasformati in lanci effettivi più avanti nel tempo, entro il time bucket stabilito per il lancio" (they become actual releases later, within the time bucket set for release). It uses the Vollmann "top handle assembly [13122]" exercise with 10-week records that have no PD column. The deck has 37 slides. | "La gestione a fabbisogno – Il Material Requirement Planning (MRP)", Corso di Logistica Industriale | Prof. Andrea Grassi, Ph.D., Università di Napoli Federico II (docenti.unina.it) | 2021 | accessed 2026-11-02 | https://www.docenti.unina.it/webdocenti-be/allegati/materiale-didattico/34433111 | confidence: high (verbatim, in Italian, my translation) | class: university course slides | secondhand. The deck's bibliography cites Vollmann, Berry & Whybark (1997), *Manufacturing Planning & Control Systems*, 4th ed., McGraw-Hill.

5. **Q3/Q1: action bucket, action notice, offset into the past.** These come from a SlideServe deck titled "Using the MRP system", uploaded by "marly" in 2012. The deck carries a "© McGraw-Hill" notice and appears to be the publisher's instructor slides for the chapter "Using the MRP System" in Vollmann et al., *Manufacturing Planning and Control Systems* (McGraw-Hill).    - bottom-up replanning, e.g. an immediate order to a vendor that has promised a shorter lead time.

## Disagreements

- **How a past-due release is shown.** The UH/Gardner model has no past-due column: the release drops off the grid and a "Not enough leadtime" error message appears (#1). The UNCW record has a "PD" column, but in its example it holds starting inventory, not releases (#2). The PD-column snippet (#6) and the Vollmann-derived slides (#5) treat it as a condition to act on: past-due bucket, offset into the past, compress lead time. None of the sources I verified places the release in period 1.
- **Who fixes it.** Gardner says to change the MPS or "find some other way". The Vollmann-derived slides (unverified) say to compress lead times or replan bottom-up with suppliers.
- **The horizon floor.** It is stated as internal throughput time (UNINA) or as cumulative lead time (unverified slm.mba). No verified source states the consequence for lowest-level items.

## Looked for, could not find

- An open, verified statement of where the planned order **receipt** sits, and how projected on hand is computed, when the release is past due. Gardner implies the receipt stays in the need week but does not say so.
- A verified, openly accessible definition of "action bucket" or "action notice". The only source was an unauthorized copy of McGraw-Hill slides (#5).
- An explicit rule linking the planning horizon to cumulative lead time, and what happens to lowest-level items if the horizon is too short, from a verified academic source.
- LibreTexts and OpenStax MRP chapters, and legitimate Jacobs & Chase or Heizer publisher slides. I ran out of budget before searching for these.
