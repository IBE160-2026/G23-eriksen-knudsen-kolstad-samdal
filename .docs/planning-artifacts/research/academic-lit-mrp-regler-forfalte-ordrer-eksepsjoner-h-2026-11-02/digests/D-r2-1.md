# D-r2-1: ASCM/APICS Dictionary definitions of MRP terms

Researcher digest, accessed 2026-11-02.

**Primary source used for all findings:** *ASCM Supply Chain Dictionary*, 18th edition (Eighteenth edition, 2024). Editors: Ann K. Gatewood and Matthew J. Drake. Copyright 2024 Association for Supply Chain Management Inc, d/b/a APICS, ASCM. It is a 214-page PDF (InDesign, created 2024-09-16), publicly downloadable from ASCM's own learning portal:

## Findings

1. **rescheduling assumption** | "A fundamental assumption of material requirements planning logic that existing open orders can be rescheduled in nearer time periods far more easily than new orders can be released and received. As a result, planned order receipts are not created until all scheduled receipts have been applied to cover gross requirements." | 18th ed., p. 164 | ASCM | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-02 | URL above | high | primary.
   - **Brief check: CONFIRMED.** The entry says MRP assumes that existing open orders can be rescheduled in more easily than new orders can be released and received. It also states the consequence: planned order receipts are not created until all scheduled receipts have been applied.
2. **action message** | "An output of a system that identifies the need for, and the type of action to be taken to correct, a current or potential problem. Examples of action messages in a material requirements planning) system include release order, reschedule in, reschedule out, and cancel. Syns.: exception …" | 18th ed., p. 7 | ASCM | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-02 | URL above | high (text after "Syns.: exception" was not captured; the stray ")" is in the source text) | primary.

4. **past-due order** (hyphenated headword) | "A line item on an open customer order that has an original scheduled ship date that is earlier than the current date. Syns.: delinquent order, late order, backorder. See: backlog." | 18th ed., P section (exact page not captured; it lies between p. 127 "open order" and p. 140 "planned order") | ASCM | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-02 | URL above | high (text); medium (page) | primary.

9. **projected available balance (PAB)** | "An inventory balance projected into the future. It is the running sum of on-hand inventory minus requirements plus scheduled receipts and planned orders. 10. **planning horizon** | "The amount of time a plan extends into the future. For a master schedule, this is normally set to cover a minimum of cumulative lead time plus time for lot sizing low-level components and time for capacity changes of primary work centers or of key suppliers. For longer-term plans, the planning horizon must be long enough to permit any needed additions to capacity. See: cumulative lead time, planning time fence." | 18th ed., p. 141 | ASCM | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-02 | URL above | high | primary.
11. **cumulative lead time** | "The longest planned length of time to accomplish the activity in question. It is found by reviewing the lead time for each bill of material path below the item. Whichever path adds up to the greatest number defines cumulative lead time. Syns.: aggregate lead time, combined lead time, composite lead time, critical path lead time, stacked lead time. See: planning horizon, planning time fence." | 18th ed., p. 47 | ASCM | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-02 | URL above | high | primary.
12. **critical path lead time** | "Syn.: cumulative lead time." | 18th ed., p. 45 | ASCM | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-02 | URL above | high | primary.
13. **low-level code** | "A number that identifies the lowest level in any bill of material at which a particular component appears. Net requirements for a given component are not calculated until all the gross requirements have been calculated down to that level. Low-level codes are normally calculated and maintained automatically by the computer software. Syn.: explosion level." | 18th ed., p. 109 | ASCM | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-02 | URL above | high | primary.
14. **planning time fence** | "A point in time denoted in the planning horizon of the master scheduling process that marks a boundary inside of which changes to the schedule may adversely affect component schedules, capacity plans, customer deliveries, and cost. Outside the planning time fence, customer orders can be booked, and changes to the master schedule can be made within the constraints of the production plan. Changes inside the planning time fence must be made …" | 18th ed., p. 141 | ASCM | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-02 | URL above | high (the rest of the entry is truncated) | primary.
15. **nervousness** | "In a material requirements planning system, a state in which minor changes in higher-level (e.g., level 0 or 1) records or the master production schedule cause significant timing or quantity changes in lower-level (e.g., level 5 or 6) schedules and orders. Syn.: system nervousness." | 18th ed., p. 122 | ASCM | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-02 | URL above | high | primary.

16. **bottom-up replanning** | "In material requirements planning, the process of using pegging data to solve material availability problems or other problems. This process is accomplished by the planner (not the computer system), who evaluates the effects of possible solutions. Pegging can be thought of as active where-used information. See: requirements traceability." | 18th ed., p. 137 | ASCM | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-02 | URL above | high | primary.

## Disagreements

- **Scope of "past-due order":** the 18th edition ties past due to a customer order line whose original scheduled ship date is before the current date. It lists "backorder" as a synonym. It has no MRP-supply-side definition (an open order or scheduled receipt whose due date has passed). Any project usage of "forfalt ordre" for supply orders is therefore an extension, not the dictionary sense.
- **Synonyms:** "critical path lead time" is a synonym of cumulative lead time in this dictionary. It does not mean the project-management (CPM) sense.
- No other source was retrieved, so this digest compares no definitions across editions. Wording in earlier editions (9th–16th) may differ, but that was not verified.
