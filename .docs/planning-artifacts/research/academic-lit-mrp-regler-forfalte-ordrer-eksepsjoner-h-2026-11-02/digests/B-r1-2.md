# Digest B-r1-2: ERP exception/action messages (B4/B5) and MRP nervousness research (B6)

Researcher run 2026-11-02. Budget: 15 tool calls, 4 sources fetched in full (2x SAP Help 4.6C, Microsoft Learn, IDEAS/RePEc). Remaining items come from search-result snippets only and are labelled that way.

## Findings

1. SAP: the rescheduling check works on **existing firmed receipts**. "The rescheduling check is used to change the dates of already existing firmed receipts." A new procurement proposal comes only when existing receipts do not cover the shortage: when bringing a receipt forward, "the system will only create another procurement proposal if the receipt quantity is not sufficient". 2. SAP: the rescheduling horizon is the window for bringing receipts forward. It is set in Customizing per plant or MRP group, and "The rescheduling horizon is always calculated from the MRP date." | same as 1 | high | erp-behaviour | primary

## Disagreements between sources

- **Freezing cost effect.** Sridharan et al. (1987, single-level MPS) find freezing up to 50 % has only a *marginal* cost effect. Zhao & Lee (multi-level MRP) find more freezing *reduces* both cost and instability and say this contradicts Sridharan et al. Blackburn et al. (1986) call freezing "dominant" together with change cost. All three agree freezing is not harmful. They disagree on whether it is cost-neutral or cost-reducing.
- **Where the horizon applies.** SAP limits *reschedule-in* proposals to the rescheduling horizon (from the MRP date). D365 limits *which orders get messages* via the freeze time fence and action-message time fence, plus day margins. The concepts are similar but scoped differently. SAP horizon defaults (one forum says 999 days) are contested in forum threads.
- **Release gap.** SAP sources are R/3 4.6C help pages. Current S/4HANA behaviour, including the newer MRP Live, was not verified.
